Food Classifier - Accuracy Analysis and Future Improvements
===========================================================

Current State (Phase 5):
The Dish-Wise Hierarchical Classifier (26 classes) trained on the YOLO-segmented Arabic dataset achieved a validation accuracy of ~72.77%.

Why 72.77% is a Strong Result:
------------------------------
1. Exponential Increase in Task Difficulty: 
   Moving from a binary (2-class) Veg vs. Non-Veg problem to a 26-class problem drastically reduces the baseline random-guessing accuracy from 50% to roughly 3.8%. Achieving ~73% on 26 classes proves the model is learning deep visual representations.

2. High Visual Ambiguity (Inter-Class Similarity): 
   Many Arabic dishes share identical core ingredients and visual textures. For example, 'Mandi', 'Meat Madfoon', and 'Meat Maqluba' all consist of roasted meat served over yellow/brown spiced rice. The model struggles to differentiate the microscopic textual differences of the rice or meat preparation without massive amounts of data.

3. Limited Data per Class:
   With approximately 1,439 images across 26 classes, the dataset averages about 55 images per dish. Deep learning models typically require hundreds or thousands of raw, unique images per class for fine-grained classification.

4. True Generalization vs. Data Leakage:
   Prior to training, strict Perceptual Hashing (threshold=5) was applied to cluster duplicated or heavily augmented images together. By keeping these clusters within the same split (Train or Validation), we prevented data leakage. Without this strict hashing, validation accuracy might have artificially inflated to 90%+, but it would have been a "fake" score resulting from the model memorizing exact duplicates. 72.77% represents the model's true, real-world generalization capability on unseen data.


Actionable Future Improvements to Reach 90%+ Accuracy:
------------------------------------------------------
1. Dataset Expansion (Data Centric):
   - Gather significantly more raw, unaugmented images per dish. The target should be at least 200-300 unique images per class.
   - Introduce harder negative mining by explicitly collecting images of visually similar dishes that the model currently confuses.

2. Architectural Upgrades (Model Centric):
   - Upgrade from MobileNetV3 (a lightweight, mobile-optimized architecture) to a heavier, high-capacity model such as ConvNeXt (Base/Large) or a Vision Transformer (ViT).
   - Heavier architectures possess larger receptive fields and parameter counts, allowing them to capture the subtle textural nuances (e.g., the specific grain of Mandi rice) required for fine-grained classification.

3. Two-Stage Hierarchical Training:
   - Train a dedicated model solely for separating highly confused subsets (e.g., a sub-model that only classifies between Mandi, Madfoon, and Maqluba) once the primary model predicts the broad "Rice and Meat" category.
